Communication

Voice & Tone

We Do
  • Write in short, direct sentences with no filler
  • Use confident, active language that commands attention
  • Lead with the benefit, not the feature
  • Keep copy tight — every word earns its place
  • Sound like a trusted friend, not a corporation
  • Celebrate simplicity and routine-building
We Don’t
  • Use fluffy, vague, or overpromising language
  • Write long paragraphs — men skim, keep it scannable
  • Make false claims or use gimmick-driven messaging
  • Sound clinical, technical, or like a whitepaper
  • Use passive voice or hedge with “may help” language
  • Overcomplicate the routine or the messaging
